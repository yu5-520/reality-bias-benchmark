# Open Research Protocol

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2026-09-22  
**Project:** Reality Bias Benchmark / Process Reality research programme  
**Maintainer:** Yeyu Zheng

> **Open for use. Clear in provenance. Responsible in evolution.**

## 1. Purpose

This repository is openly published to support:

- replication;
- independent validation;
- criticism;
- alternative adjudication;
- modification and extension;
- new experiments;
- new implementations;
- teaching and research;
- downstream scientific and engineering work.

The purpose of this protocol is not to create artificial barriers around an open research programme. It is to make the project's provenance, stewardship, reuse expectations, and evolution rules explicit.

## 2. Openness does not erase provenance

Public availability does not waive or erase the historical provenance, authorship, or priority of the original research framework, experimental architecture, concepts, protocols, evidence organization, or engineering designs developed in this repository.

Anyone may independently test, criticize, extend, reimplement, or build on the work under the applicable licences.

Where this repository materially informs later scholarly work, normal academic practice is expected: identify relevant prior work and cite it appropriately.

This is a scholarly norm, not a technical access gate.

## 3. Reuse and independent implementation

Replication and independent implementation do not require prior permission when they are permitted by the applicable licence.

Downstream researchers are encouraged to:

- state which repository version, release, protocol, or evidence set they used;
- distinguish direct reuse from independent reimplementation;
- report modifications that materially change experimental geometry or interpretation;
- preserve links to the upstream provenance when practical;
- publish negative, contradictory, or boundary-setting results rather than only confirming results.

Independent implementations are welcome. They should not imply that they are the official implementation, endorsed implementation, or canonical extension unless that status has been explicitly agreed.

## 4. Research lineage and citation

The project treats citation as part of scholarly provenance rather than as a mechanism for controlling access.

If the repository, its associated paper, experimental framework, architecture, concepts, methods, or evidence materially contribute to academic work, citation of the relevant source is encouraged and consistent with normal scholarly practice.

The repository provides a machine-readable citation entry in `CITATION.cff`.

When the associated paper receives a stable publication identifier, the preferred citation may be updated prospectively without rewriting earlier repository history.

## 5. Reciprocal research map

Research lineage is not intended to be one-way.

High-quality downstream work that replicates, challenges, extends, operationalizes, or substantially develops this research direction may be listed in `ECOSYSTEM.md`.

Relevant independent work may also be listed even if it does not cite this repository.

The purpose of the ecosystem map is to help readers discover the growing research field, not to reward citation mechanically.

Inclusion does not imply endorsement, agreement, authorship, ownership, or validation by the maintainer.

## 6. Evidence integrity

The repository already uses append-only and frozen-evidence principles. Open contribution does not override them.

Canonical rules include:

- frozen raw evidence is not silently rewritten;
- corrections are appended and versioned;
- historical interpretations remain inspectable;
- later theory does not retroactively alter raw historical records;
- superseded files may remain for auditability;
- hashes, manifests, and evidence identities are preserved where available;
- new semantic interpretation is distinguished from new experimental execution.

A contribution that changes interpretation should not rewrite the underlying historical evidence to match the new interpretation.

## 7. Scientific status boundaries

Contributors and downstream users are encouraged to preserve distinctions between:

- observation;
- mechanism evidence;
- retrospective interpretation;
- prospective validation;
- engineering demonstration;
- hypothesis;
- application outlook;
- external validation;
- established result.

The repository's open status should not be used to inflate claim strength.

Likewise, external reuse should not imply that a concept has been independently validated merely because it has been implemented.

## 8. Framework evolution and compatibility

The project is designed as a research programme rather than a single frozen software product.

New work may add:

- alternative semantic auditors;
- new model families;
- new agent topologies;
- new domains;
- new lineage representations;
- new Process Reality dimensions or refinements;
- new monitor/repair implementations;
- new conformance tests;
- new application branches.

Where a downstream project claims compatibility with a specific Process Reality, Dynamic CPR, Semantic Lineage, or Semantic Process Integrity interface, it is encouraged to identify the version or contract used.

Compatibility should be demonstrated by interface or test behavior rather than by name alone.

## 9. Contributions and stewardship

The maintainer's responsibility is not to control all downstream work.

It is to keep the upstream research source legible:

- preserve chronology;
- maintain clear definitions;
- separate evidence from interpretation;
- version material changes;
- keep interfaces inspectable;
- record important boundary changes;
- avoid silently rewriting history;
- make it possible for later researchers to trace where an idea, protocol, or evidence object entered the project.

Contributors retain credit for their contributions. Contribution does not automatically create authorship on any paper; scholarly authorship is determined by actual research contribution to the relevant work.

See `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

## 10. Commercial activity

This protocol does not create a blanket non-commercial restriction.

Commercial research, implementation, and deployment remain governed by the applicable licences and third-party terms.

Organizations interested in substantial implementation, deployment, standardization, or long-term development of the original architecture are welcome to contact the maintainer for collaboration.

The project prefers adoption with clear provenance over artificial barriers to use.

## 11. Third-party material

Open publication cannot grant rights the project does not own.

Third-party software, provider/model outputs, quoted literature, external datasets, trademarks, and other externally sourced material remain subject to their own terms and applicable law.

See `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` and `LICENSES.md`.

## 12. No endorsement and no warranty

Listing a downstream project, paper, company, implementation, or contributor does not imply endorsement.

The repository is research infrastructure and is provided without warranty to the extent permitted by the applicable licences.

## 13. Protocol evolution

This protocol may evolve as the research programme grows.

Changes should be:

- prospective;
- visible in version history;
- explicit about changed rights or expectations;
- non-destructive to historical provenance.

The project should not silently reinterpret earlier releases as if later governance rules had always existed.

---

## Short form

**Use it. Test it. Challenge it. Extend it.**

If the work materially informs later research, preserve the scholarly lineage.

If the field grows beyond this repository, that is a success condition rather than a loss of control.
