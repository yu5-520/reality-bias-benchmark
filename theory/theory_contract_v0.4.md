## 0. Scope

The first paper studies Reality Bias as a system-level trajectory phenomenon in a minimal AI-driven multi-agent environment.

The program does not claim:

- direct access to hidden chain-of-thought or neural state;
- that visible reasoning text is a faithful record of internal computation;
- that every model error is Reality Bias;
- that Tension or Escape Propensity is directly observable;
- that C/P/R exhaust all possible future Reality Bias phenomena;
- that any one orchestration architecture is universally safer.

The primary evidence is recorded system behavior and state transition.

## 1. Measurement ontology

The forward ontology separates four levels.

### 1.1 System Trajectory — experimental / analysis unit

A complete subject run or branch continuation is a system trajectory:

`T = (S0, B0, S1, B1, ... , Sn)`

System-level claims concern trajectory properties such as first Jump, propagation, operational penetration, inherited inertia, retrospective stabilization and recovery.

### 1.2 Node / Boundary — measurement location

A node or boundary is a location where system behavior can be recorded or experimentally manipulated, including Agent turns, shared-state operations, message handoff, invocation, commit, FINAL/reopen and authority conversion.

Nodes are measurement locations, not independent proof that a Reality Bias occurred.

### 1.3 Behavior / State Transition — primary observable

A Behavior Event is an externally recorded action such as:

`read / write / message / invoke / commit / reopen / revise / inherit / finalize`

A State Transition is the recorded consequence:

`S_t → S_{t+1}`

The program prioritizes behavior over reconstruction of the model's full reasoning process.

### 1.4 Semantic Annotation — post-hoc interpretation layer

C/P/R, semantic adoption, laundering, normalization, decision effect and some Authority conditions are semantic/contractual judgments applied to frozen behavior evidence.

Reviewer disagreement cannot rewrite structural behavior records.

## 2. Unified mechanism chain

The forward mechanism chain is:

`controllable antecedents X_pre`
`→ structural/interaction Tension`
`→ latent Escape Propensity`
`→ observable Jump realization`
`→ first-order C/P state-deviation candidate`
`→ adoption / commit`
`→ propagation / Authority Penetration / inherited inertia`
`→ challenge / correction / retrospective operation`
`→ second-order R dynamics or recovery`
`→ system outcome`

The ordering after Jump need not be strictly linear. Propagation, penetration, feedback and inertia may recur or interact.

## 3. Tension

**Tension** is a theoretical construct describing conflict or competition among task-relevant goals, evidence, context, constraints, authority and available actions.

Examples of manipulable antecedents that may alter Tension include:

- goal conflict;
- evidence conflict;
- context competition;
- authority mismatch;
- information load;
- alternative path availability.

Tension is not itself a Bias and does not imply a Jump.

The first paper does not require direct measurement of Tension. It may manipulate antecedent variables and observe behavioral outcomes.

## 4. Escape Propensity

**Escape Propensity** is a latent tendency for locally plausible synthesis or action selection to produce a task-relevant displacement outside the previously supported or authorized closure.

Escape Propensity is not directly read from chain-of-thought or hidden model state.

A behavior-level hazard may be estimated under repeated frozen conditions:

`h_t = P(J_t = 1 | recorded state/context/goal/authority condition at t)`

This estimates repeated behavior, not a neural probability.

## 5. Jump

A **Jump** is the observable realization of a task-relevant displacement in recorded system behavior or state transition.

Candidate Jump dimensions include:

- epistemic status / provenance;
- goal scope / focus;
- invocation / task graph;
- settled/final/historical state;
- authority-bearing state;
- other preregistered state dimensions.

A structural Jump candidate is not automatically a semantic Reality Bias.

Jump detection and Jump explanation are separate tasks:

- detection locates the recorded transition;
- explanation tests which variables alter the probability or downstream consequence of that transition.

## 6. Generation, Realization and Penetration

**Bias Generation** occurs when the model/system proposes a beyond-contract inference, scope expansion, action or reinterpretation.

**Bias Realization** occurs when generated content changes an effective system state or executable path.

Therefore:

`reasoning/proposal ≠ operational reality`

and:

`propagation ≠ Authority Penetration`

**Authority Penetration** is the process by which Jump-derived content progressively acquires operational force without satisfying the required end-to-end Authority condition.

A visible or transmitted deviation may fail to penetrate.

## 7. Inherited Inertia

**Inherited Inertia** is path dependence after a Jump-derived state becomes part of downstream context, state, goals, evidence or execution history.

A later Agent can reason locally correctly relative to an already displaced state.

Thus:

> escape occurs at the Jump; inertia appears after the Jump becomes inherited context.

Candidate metrics include descendant count, affected actors, persistence length and preregistered survival-like functions.

## 8. C and P as first-order semantic forms

### 8.1 Completion Bias (C)

C is a first-order epistemic-state deviation in which missing, uncertain, weakly supported or provisional information is promoted beyond its authorized status.

Example path:

`uncertain / inferred / provisional → stronger epistemic status → inherited as reality`

Explicitly provisional, non-inheritable inference is not C-Realization.

### 8.2 Perfection Bias (P)

P is a first-order goal/scope/focus deviation in which the system introduces unnecessary or unauthorized expansion of purpose, scope, information surface, Agent/tool/stage surface or decision focus.

Example paths:

`G0 → G0 + ΔG`

or:

`same nominal goal → unauthorized material focus shift`

Necessary decomposition and explicitly authorized change are exclusions.

## 9. R as second-order dynamics

R is not ordinary reopen, revision, rework or correction.

R requires:

`prior C/P deviation or unresolved C/P-derived state`
`+ retrospective/challenge/correction opportunity`
`+ persistence/regeneration/amplification/legitimation/laundering/normalization`

Possible outcomes include:

- `CORRECTION`;
- `PERSISTENCE`;
- `REGENERATION`;
- `AMPLIFICATION`;
- `LAUNDERING`;
- `NORMALIZATION`.

Correction is recovery evidence, not R-positive evidence.

## 10. Recovery

Recovery is restoration of an authorized and evidence-consistent system trajectory after a Jump-derived deviation is detected or challenged.

Candidate measurements include:

- residual descendants;
- recurrence/regeneration;
- distance to last valid state;
- recovery turns/calls/tokens;
- provenance reconstruction fidelity;
- remaining operational contamination.

## 11. Behavior-first evidence principle

The default evidence chain is:

`Behavior Event → State Transition → structural detector → lineage/dynamics → small semantic review window`

not:

`full reasoning transcript → Reviewer searches for Bias`

Natural-language reasoning may be retained as supporting evidence, but the experiment does not require reconstructing the model's complete internal thought process.

## 12. Causal experimentation

The theory supports intervention at multiple positions.

### 12.1 PRE — Formation

Manipulate antecedent variables before Jump realization and observe Jump incidence / location / type.

### 12.2 MID — Realization / Containment

Manipulate status, authority routes, commit gates, routing edges, shared-state visibility or equivalent variables and observe propagation / penetration / inertia.

### 12.3 POST — Stabilization

Manipulate challenge timing, provenance restoration, correction/reopen policy or verification timing and observe persistence / R / recovery.

Preferred causal form remains:

`same frozen parent + one preregistered intervention → branched continuation`

where scientifically and operationally valid.

## 13. System structure as an experimental variable

Topology and orchestration are not merely implementation details.

Relevant variables include:

- routing ownership;
- topology density;
- stage boundaries;
- context continuity/reset/compression;
- dynamic invocation surface;
- proposal/commit separation;
- Agent count;
- shared-state visibility.

Fixed topology does not imply fixed semantic state.

## 14. R2–R4 simultaneous observation principle

R2, R3 and R4 are not required to be separate sequential subject experiments.

One frozen trajectory can simultaneously support:

- R2 Behavior / Jump observations;
- R3 propagation / penetration / inertia observations;
- R4 retrospective windows.

Semantic review may occur asynchronously after the subject evidence is frozen.

## 15. Falsifiability

The forward theory is weakened if adequate preregistered experiments show that:

- proposed Jump definitions do not produce reproducible behavior/state-transition events;
- manipulating antecedent variables fails to change any predicted Jump-related behavior across adequate tests;
- propagation cannot be separated from operational penetration;
- Jump-derived descendants show no distinguishable path dependence from matched comparisons;
- R cannot be separated from ordinary correction/rework;
- targeted MID interventions do not alter predicted downstream structure;
- recovery does not differ with distance/commit/lineage conditions where predicted;
- system-structure manipulations do not affect any preregistered trajectory quantity.

Null results remain valid evidence.
