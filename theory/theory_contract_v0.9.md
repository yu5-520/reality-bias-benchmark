## 0. Scope

The program studies **Process Reality Fidelity** in multi-Agent systems: how information acquires process authority, enters downstream carriers, forms inherited system inertia, responds to bounded local perturbation, and can later be distinguished from natural variability and generic uncertainty before localized recovery or semantic adjudication.

Terminal task correctness remains a separate outcome axis.

## 1. Evidence layers

Keep five evidentiary objects distinct:

1. raw observable facts;
2. source-backed structural derivation;
3. intervention/local-response evidence;
4. inertia-identification evidence;
5. semantic CPR adjudication after structural freeze.

Terminal outcome is reported separately from all five.

No one structural difference may be used as a shortcut to establish every later layer.

## 2. Jump

A Jump is an observable local displacement in recorded behavior/state that changes a preregistered reality-bearing dimension such as epistemic status, scope/focus, executable path, settled state or authority/responsibility state.

A Jump is an event. It is not automatically an error and not automatically semantic C/P/R.

## 3. Reality Authority

**Reality Authority** is the process-level status by which information is treated as sufficiently factual, settled, authorized or provenance-supported to influence later reasoning, messages, state, invocations or historical process structure.

Reality Authority is not merely a confidence word. It is operational when downstream system behavior treats an information item as if that authority were available.

An information item may be factually correct yet still acquire authority without adequate support at the time of use. Conversely, a later incorrect outcome does not by itself prove an earlier authority violation.

## 4. Inherited System Inertia

**Inherited System Inertia** is source-backed path dependence in which prior process state affects later process structure through observable carriers.

Possible carriers include:

- message content and provenance;
- state field/revision;
- invocation request/result;
- evidence/source reference;
- inherited constraint/goal;
- final/reopen/revision history;
- Agent/stage handoff.

Inertia requires more than activity volume or graph reachability. The strongest evidence shows that a carrier is delivered/read, adopted in a later decision, inherited into a new state/action, and possibly propagated again.

Jump phenotype may disappear while structural inertia remains.

## 5. Carrier evidence levels

Use the following non-semantic evidence ladder:

1. `REACHABLE_CARRIER` — downstream structural path exists;
2. `DELIVERED_OR_READ_CARRIER` — the downstream node actually received/read it;
3. `ADOPTED_CARRIER` — later behavior explicitly uses/references it;
4. `INHERITED_CARRIER` — a later state/action is source-backed by it;
5. `PROPAGATED_CARRIER` — that inherited state/action affects further descendants.

Structural reachability is not equivalent to semantic adoption.

## 6. R2-R6 geometry

R2, R3, R4 and R6 remain analytical views over realized trajectories. R5 is the bounded perturbation operator.

- R2: natural Jump emergence;
- R3: inheritance/propagation structure;
- R4: challenge/reopen/retrospective dynamics;
- R5: one-shot local perturbation;
- R6: **System Inertia Identification**.

R6 now contains four identification responsibilities:

- R6-A natural inertia baseline;
- R6-B carrier/inheritance identification;
- R6-C intervention-related inertia transition;
- R6-D target specificity.

Thus:

> **R5 perturbs; R6 determines what actually persisted or changed, how it was carried, whether the change exceeds natural variability, and whether it is specific to the targeted J0-derived information.**

## 7. R5 — bounded authority-withdrawal probe

The canonical R5 family withdraws acquired factual authority once, for example:

`fact -> unconfirmed`

R5 is defined by its limits:

- no opposite factual claim;
- no replacement conclusion;
- no desired action;
- no desired terminal answer;
- no Agent identity or role change;
- no topology/path prescription;
- no persistent experiment-origin mutation;
- no reinjection after direct exposure;
- free continuation after consumption.

R5 primary property: **local perturbability / direct path response**.

R5 does not alone establish system-inertia change, condition effect, target specificity, semantic bias or recovery.

## 8. Direct response versus post-consumption inertia

The direct exposure window must be separated from later inertia observation.

Examples of local response:

- immediate verification;
- one extra write;
- one extra message;
- one invocation;
- immediate reroute;
- early finalization/termination.

These can be real effects yet remain local.

R6 inertia claims require source-backed consequences after the experiment-origin signal has exited active context.

## 9. R6-A — natural variability baseline

Natural repeated continuations define the reference geometry of the system.

Large natural variation is not merely a limitation; it is part of the phenomenon that must be measured before attributing a difference to intervention.

Conceptually compare:

- `d(Ai, Aj)` — natural-condition variability;
- `d(Bi, Bj)` — treated-condition variability;
- `d(Ai, Bj)` — cross-condition variability.

`d` must be preregistered as a process-distance vector or frozen aggregation rule. It must not be invented after seeing the desired outcome.

A single different A/B trajectory is not sufficient to establish an intervention effect when natural trajectories already diverge strongly.

## 10. R6-B — carrier identification

R6 must identify not only that later structure differs but **what carried historical influence forward**.

A valid inertia account should, where evidence permits, trace:

`prior information/state -> message/state/invocation carrier -> downstream read/use -> new state/action -> later descendant`

Carrier tracing combines graph lineage with bounded semantic-use evidence. Neither alone is always sufficient.

## 11. R6-C — intervention-related inertia transition

An intervention-related inertia transition is supported only when downstream differences after direct exposure are distinguishable from the system's natural variability under the frozen comparison design.

No monotonic complexity direction is required.

Possible transition forms include:

- attenuation;
- amplification;
- deflection;
- transfer;
- transformation;
- reconstruction;
- delayed re-emergence;
- altered re-entry/branch/merge topology;
- altered path-family distribution;
- changed reconvergence.

Natural termination is censoring, not proof of extinction.

## 12. R6-D — target specificity

Target specificity is an internal R6 identification problem.

Use:

- S0 Natural Reference;
- S1 ordinary/matched factual information downgrade;
- S2 J0-targeted escape-derived factual-authority withdrawal.

Interpretations:

- `S1 - S0` = generic uncertainty response;
- `S2 - S0` = total J0-targeted response;
- `S2 - S1` = target-specificity contrast.

Primary specificity readout: **post-consumption system-inertia profile**.

S1 need not have zero local or downstream effect.

## 13. Exact-source atomic specificity

The exact frozen R5-MID parent has no second non-J0 top-level `fact` metadata state. Therefore the forward specificity design uses atomic factual information rather than forcing an invalid top-level match.

Frozen prospective targets:

- S1: `public_context.products.C.gross_margin_pct = 35`;
- S2: `shared_state.inventory_stockout_assessment_v1.A.preliminary_stock = 1520`.

The S2 source signal originated as `preliminary_unreconciled` and later entered a container with acquired `fact` status at J0.

Both arms use the same minimal one-shot target-scoped epistemic annotation. Original values remain unchanged.

## 14. CPR semantic taxonomy

CPR remains a downstream semantic adjudication layer.

### C

A weak/limited evidential proposition acquires stronger inheritable factual authority without sufficient support, and downstream use reflects that stronger authority.

A `fact` label alone is insufficient.

### P

Goal/scope/focus/information/Agent/tool/stage expansion exceeds the supported or authorized boundary and materially changes the process.

An extra Agent/tool call alone is insufficient.

### R

A prior C/P-derived deviation or descendant persists, regenerates, normalizes or is re-justified after a valid challenge/correction opportunity, remains source-linked to the prior deviation, and lacks sufficient new evidence for re-confirmation.

Same-key recurrence alone is insufficient.

## 15. R7 — risk-bounded structural control

R7 asks whether an identified risk-bearing inertia carrier/closure can be localized and repaired while preserving unrelated process freedom.

R7 conditions remain C1/C2/C3 and are orthogonal to the S specificity namespace.

R7 success requires both:

- risk reduction/localization;
- preservation of unaffected nodes/fields/branches/Agent participation/path diversity.

ALR remains Authority-Localized Recovery: repair the violated authority/provenance condition and affected dependency closure, not the terminal answer.

## 16. Engineering implication of R7

The theory naturally motivates a **Process Integrity Protocol** for multi-Agent runtimes.

This is an engineering implication, not a replacement for the R7 scientific hypothesis.

Desired properties:

- lightweight;
- pluggable;
- protocol-agnostic;
- passive/non-invasive by default;
- real-time;
- content-addressed;
- lineage-aware;
- locally recoverable;
- auditable/reversible.

The normal mode is observation and indexing, not orchestration.

Minimal lifecycle:

`observe -> content-address -> trace lineage -> detect/localize -> compute affected closure -> point repair if authorized -> resume -> verify lineage`

The protocol must not become a central supervisor that manufactures the topology it claims to observe.

## 17. Content addressing

Content/revision addressing allows the runtime to bind event/state/message/field/source/revision identities at creation time and later trace transformations even when natural-language wording changes.

Engineering identity may include:

- EventHash;
- MessageHash;
- StateHash;
- FieldHash;
- SourceHash;
- RevisionHash;
- parent/lineage refs.

This supports real-time localization and post-repair verification of old/new lineage.

## 18. Falsifiability

The program is weakened if adequate prospective evidence shows that:

- J0 cannot be localized reproducibly;
- the R5 operator cannot be separated from prompt steering/persistent mutation;
- local response cannot be separated from post-consumption inheritance;
- claimed carriers lack source-backed read/use/inheritance evidence;
- natural variability is comparable to or larger than claimed condition effects with no repeatable condition structure;
- S2 is not distinguishable from S1 under adequate matched replication;
- specificity depends on post-outcome control selection;
- ALR requires whole-system suppression despite claims of local recovery;
- content-addressed lineage does not improve localization beyond ordinary logs under the tested setting;
- semantic CPR conclusions cannot be supported independently of structural change.

## 19. Nonclaims

The forward theory does not claim in advance that:

- every Jump is a Reality Bias event;
- every ordinary fact should be behaviorally inert when marked uncertain;
- R5 path difference proves long-range inertia change;
- R6 structural difference proves CPR;
- S2 must be larger or more complex than S1;
- one parent establishes population-level causality;
- provider hidden state is replayed;
- termination proves extinction;
- an engineering protocol must replace existing Agent/MCP/A2A interfaces;
- R7 engineering value is already scientifically validated by readiness tests.
