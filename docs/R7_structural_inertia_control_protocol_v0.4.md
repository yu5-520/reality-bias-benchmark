## 1. Purpose

R7 tests whether a **source-backed, risk-bearing inherited-inertia carrier or affected dependency closure** can be localized, bounded or repaired while preserving unrelated process freedom.

R7 is a scientific control/recovery question. Its product/engineering implication is a lightweight **Process Integrity Protocol**, documented separately in `docs/R7_process_integrity_engineering_profile_v0.1.md`.

The engineering profile does not replace the scientific R7 claim and is not automatically validated by R7 readiness evidence.

## 2. Entry requirement from R6

R7 should not treat every path difference as a repair target.

A valid R7 target should be supported by R6 evidence identifying, where possible:

- natural J0 / source root;
- carrier or affected lineage;
- downstream inheritance/propagation evidence;
- intervention-related or naturally persistent inertia structure;
- risk-relevant authority/provenance condition;
- censoring limits.

If the risk-bearing carrier/closure is unresolved, R7 may test localization failure but must not pretend the target is known.

## 3. R7 conditions

The `C` namespace remains reserved for R7 structural-handling conditions.

### C1 — One-Shot Free Continuation

The direct experiment-origin correction/authority-withdrawal signal is exposed once and then consumed. Downstream process continues freely.

Scientific role: observe endogenous inherited inertia after the signal exits.

### C2 — Persistent Field Propagation

The same semantic correction field remains experiment-visible downstream.

Scientific role: compare endogenous inertia with experiment-maintained persistence.

### C3 — ALR Authority-Localized Recovery

Use source-backed authority/provenance and dependency structure to:

- locate earliest violating ancestor;
- compute affected closure;
- preserve unaffected realized structure;
- repair the violated condition;
- create revision identity/hash;
- reopen/re-execute only the affected subgraph where runtime permits;
- release the process back to free execution.

Scientific role: test localized risk control/recovery rather than continuous forcing.

## 4. Primary contrasts

### R7-A: C1 vs C2

Question:

> What part of downstream persistence is endogenous system inertia versus persistence maintained by the experiment itself?

### R7-B: C2 vs C3

Question:

> Given materially matched correction semantics, does source-backed localized recovery reduce/localize risky inheritance while preserving more unrelated structure than continuous field propagation?

### Optional C1 vs C3

Question:

> Does localized recovery alter risk-bearing inheritance relative to free continuation while preserving unrelated process diversity?

## 5. Dual success criterion

R7 must report two outcome families simultaneously.

### Risk-control side

- risky descendant count;
- risky reach/depth;
- residual old lineage;
- recurrence/re-entry;
- secondary risk Jump;
- time/distance to containment;
- affected/reopened node count.

### Freedom-preservation side

- unaffected nodes preserved;
- unaffected fields preserved;
- unaffected branches preserved;
- unrelated Agent participation retained;
- unrelated natural path diversity retained;
- unnecessary reopen count;
- full rerun avoided/invoked.

A method that suppresses the whole process may reduce measured risk but fails the localized-preservation objective.

## 6. No target answer

R7 may repair authority/provenance conditions, visibility/propagation mode, revision lineage or reopen scope.

It may not prescribe a desired terminal answer merely to create apparent recovery.

The post-repair subject process remains generative.

## 7. Proposal-versus-realization evidence

If C3 blocks, redirects, revises or reopens structure, preserve both:

- original subject proposal/action;
- realized post-recovery action/state.

Recovery evidence must not erase attempted behavior.

## 8. Content-addressed recovery identity

Where runtime support exists, C3 should record:

- recovery operation id;
- authority/provenance condition id;
- earliest violating ancestor `n*`;
- source refs;
- affected-closure hash;
- preserved-set hash;
- prior FieldHash/StateHash/RevisionHash;
- repaired FieldHash/StateHash/RevisionHash;
- parent revision ref;
- reopened-subgraph hash;
- new descendant refs.

This supports point repair and post-repair lineage verification.

## 9. Real-time operation boundary

R7 scientific treatment may be implemented with a runtime observer/recovery layer, but passive observation must remain behaviorally inert by default.

The observer may register events and hashes in real time. It must not:

- change Agent ordering;
- rewrite normal prompts;
- force tool/Agent choice;
- block natural paths merely because a candidate Jump exists;
- adjudicate CPR automatically.

Active intervention begins only when the frozen R7 treatment condition authorizes it.

## 10. Relationship to S specificity namespace

`S` and `C` answer different questions:

- S: **which target** receives the one-shot uncertainty/authority withdrawal;
- C: **how an identified risk-bearing condition is structurally handled**.

They may reuse related mechanical primitives but must never be aliased in plans/evidence.

## 11. Engineering baselines

Where resources permit, compare C3 against:

- Full Rerun;
- Checkpoint Retry;
- persistent semantic field;
- no recovery.

Engineering metrics may include:

- latency;
- model calls;
- tokens/spend;
- preserved-node ratio;
- reopened-node count;
- recovery distance;
- recurrence.

These are useful but secondary to the core scientific R7 claim.

## 12. Scientific claim boundary

A positive R7 result may support:

> under the frozen design, source-backed localized recovery reduced or bounded the identified risk-bearing inherited structure while preserving materially more unrelated process structure than the comparator.

It does not automatically establish:

- universal controllability;
- semantic CPR truth;
- superiority across architectures/models/domains;
- commercial production readiness;
- safety guarantees.

## 13. Offline readiness

Before any real R7 subject execution, validate offline:

- same-observability capture across C arms;
- authority-condition registry;
- carrier/closure binding from R6;
- affected/preserved partition;
- content/revision identity;
- local reopen plan;
- proposal-versus-realization evidence;
- no-op regression when active recovery disabled;
- authorization/spend gate.
