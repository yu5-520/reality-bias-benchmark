## 0. Purpose and precedence

This chapter does not rewrite the frozen first Stage-II natural matrix. It converts the lessons of G1 into explicit forward rules for G2-G5.

The repository now distinguishes two peer parent chapters:

1. **Experimental General Chapter** — defines natural execution, control variables, evidence boundaries, replication, semantic audit, CPR interpretation, and group-level comparison.
2. **Engineering General Chapter** — defines monitoring, warning, checkpoint, resumability, repair eligibility, structured repair, repair-boundary watching, and post-repair validation.

The two chapters may reference each other. Neither may substitute for the other's evidence.

The frozen original 21-cell Stage-II matrix is designated:

> **G1 — discovery/calibration reference group**

The next four groups are designated:

> **G2-G5 — prospective replication groups**

G1 remains immutable. G2-G5 follow this v2 contract prospectively.

# 1. Experimental control-variable principles

## EXP-CV-01 — First-attempt-only natural execution

Each pre-registered X×T cell receives exactly one natural first attempt per group.

A failure, null terminal answer, provider error, horizon stop, framework exception, or incomplete process does not authorize an automatic second natural attempt.

Purpose: preserve stochastic first realizations instead of manufacturing reproducibility by repeated sampling.

---

## EXP-CV-02 — Group independence

G2, G3, G4, and G5 are independent prospective groups.

They are not retries of G1 and are not retries of one another.

A cell that fails in G2 still receives its separately pre-registered first attempt in G3 because G3 is a new group, not because G2 is being retried.

---

## EXP-CV-03 — Full natural matrix per prospective group

Every prospective replication group begins with the same natural observation population:

`X1-X7 × T1-T3 = 21 natural first attempts`.

No system is removed from the natural phase merely because a later repair branch is unavailable.

In particular, X1 and X3 remain full natural-observation members.

---

## EXP-CV-04 — Common task freeze

T1-T3 task definitions are frozen before G2 begins.

The same task family meaning, repository fixture, role purpose, and user-level authorization boundary apply across G2-G5.

A task may not be rewritten after observing one framework's behavior.

---

## EXP-CV-05 — Common execution-horizon rule

G2-G5 use one prospectively frozen common model-decision horizon.

G1 used a 32-turn horizon on relevant runners. Frozen G1 evidence shows that this horizon frequently censored active collaboration after substantive work had already occurred.

The forward target is a 64 logical model-decision ceiling, subject to a pre-execution equivalence check showing how each native wrapper maps its framework-native scheduling unit onto the common logical-decision budget.

The ceiling is a hard observation boundary, not a completion target.

---

## EXP-CV-06 — Horizon change may occur only between groups

A horizon may not be extended because an individual cell is close to completion.

Any future horizon revision, if scientifically necessary, must occur before the next entire group starts, be documented as a protocol version change, and then apply symmetrically to the full group.

G2-G5 are intended to keep the same frozen horizon.

---

## EXP-CV-07 — Horizon censoring is not task failure

A trajectory stopped solely because it reaches the pre-registered horizon is classified as:

`HORIZON_CENSORED_NON_CLOSURE`

unless another stronger failure cause has already terminated it.

It must not be silently counted as framework task failure.

The report must separately record:

- whether substantive work occurred;
- whether repository writes occurred;
- whether semantic completion claims occurred;
- whether process closure occurred;
- whether pending messages/work remained.

---

## EXP-CV-08 — Provider/runtime failures are separate outcome classes

Malformed provider JSON, network failure, transport failure, process crash, or equivalent infrastructure/runtime errors are recorded separately from framework task outcomes.

For example, the frozen G1 X4-T2 and X6-T2 failures remain:

`PROVIDER_JSON_RESPONSE_MALFORMED_AFTER_IDENTICAL_RETRY`

and must not be rewritten as MCP or MemoryBank task failures.

---

## EXP-CV-09 — Measurement-interface artifacts are separate outcome classes

If an experiment-defined interface prevents meaningful subject execution, that artifact must be identified separately.

The frozen G1 T1 X2-X7 row is permanently interpreted under the historical v1 action-envelope contract and is not a clean cross-framework task-performance baseline.

G2-G5 use the clarified prospective action contract rather than intentionally reproducing the known v1 measurement artifact.

---

## EXP-CV-10 — Model/runtime configuration freeze

For each prospective group, the model/provider binding, temperature/sampling settings, upstream framework versions, task fixture, roles, execution contract, monitor version, and evidence schema are frozen before the first cell runs.

Changes are versioned between groups, never made ad hoc inside a group.

---

## EXP-CV-11 — No outcome-conditioned tuning

No parameter, role, prompt, tool allowance, horizon, retry rule, repair rule, or evidence threshold may be changed because one realized trajectory looks poor, incomplete, unusual, or inconvenient.

Forward corrections are based on protocol diagnosis, not desired outcomes.

---

## EXP-CV-12 — Retry policy freeze

Provider/network retries, where technically necessary, must be frozen in advance and identical for all applicable cells.

Retries are transport recovery, not new scientific natural attempts.

A retry must repeat the same request and may not alter semantic content to obtain a cleaner answer.

---

## EXP-CV-13 — No hidden global normalization

The experiment must not force X1-X7 through one artificial execution runtime merely to simplify comparison.

Only scientific inputs and post-observation evidence representations may be normalized.

Native scheduling, protocol, tool/resource, retrieval, memory, and compression semantics remain heterogeneous.

---

## EXP-CV-14 — Observation schema is not execution schema

The unified evidence model:

`source -> carrier -> transformation -> downstream use -> consequence`

exists for observation and audit.

It must not be injected as a pre-execution universal protocol that changes how the studied system communicates or acts.

---

## EXP-CV-15 — Semantic audit occurs after evidence freeze

Natural execution completes and raw evidence is sealed before semantic audit is opened.

Semantic audit may classify or interpret frozen evidence but may not alter the trajectory, regenerate missing events, or select a different natural result.

---

## EXP-CV-16 — Outcome and process measures remain separate

At minimum, reports distinguish:

- terminal task outcome;
- process closure;
- semantic closure;
- repository/artifact closure;
- termination reason;
- provider/runtime status;
- monitor coverage.

A terminal answer does not imply process reality, and a nonterminal process does not automatically imply a bad artifact.

# 2. Experimental boundary principles

## EXP-BD-01 — G1 evidence immutability

All frozen G1 raw trajectories, manifests, audits, and checkout states remain unchanged.

No later methodological improvement permits G1 replacement.

---

## EXP-BD-02 — Historical contract preservation

Known G1 design limitations remain part of the record.

The historical T1 v1 action-envelope artifact is documented, not retroactively repaired.

---

## EXP-BD-03 — Prospective correction only

Methodological corrections discovered from G1 may be applied only to future groups.

The purpose of G1 as discovery/calibration evidence is preserved.

---

## EXP-BD-04 — Natural observation does not establish named-framework causality

A natural X×T trajectory may establish that a structure occurred in that realized process.

It does not by itself establish that the named framework caused the structure.

---

## EXP-BD-05 — Negative evidence is first-class evidence

The following must be preserved when observed:

- no CPR-like structure;
- rapid clean closure;
- memory active without inertia;
- old artifact present but verified inert;
- retrieval active but semantically static;
- compression active but effectively no-op;
- monitor candidate rejected by audit.

Negative cases constrain theory and prevent taxonomy inflation.

---

## EXP-BD-06 — No framework ranking

Stage II is not a leaderboard.

The evidence may compare documented process structures, available carriers, closure patterns, or repair eligibility.

It may not convert the five-group mechanism study into an overall “best framework” ranking.

---

## EXP-BD-07 — Probability is part of the phenomenon

Cross-group variation is not treated as noise that must be averaged away by uncontrolled reruns.

Recurrence, absence, transformation, migration, and re-entry across G1-G5 are all meaningful realized states.

---

## EXP-BD-08 — Population-prevalence boundary

Five groups increase replication strength but do not automatically justify universal population prevalence claims.

Any occurrence-rate statement must name its denominator and population:

for example, “within the 105 pre-registered natural cells” rather than “AI systems generally.”

---

## EXP-BD-09 — Missing internal semantics remain missing

The audit may infer only from exposed evidence surfaces.

Unobserved private framework state is not reconstructed as if directly measured.

---

## EXP-BD-10 — Endpoint correctness remains terminal evidence

Outcome correctness is retained as a terminal indicator.

It does not redefine the research object away from process reality.

---

# 3. Experimental architecture principles

## EXP-AR-01 — Five-group architecture

The full publication-facing experimental programme is:

- G1: frozen discovery/calibration reference;
- G2: prospective replication 1;
- G3: prospective replication 2;
- G4: prospective replication 3;
- G5: prospective replication 4.

The natural population ceiling is therefore 105 first-attempt cells if all five groups complete.

---

## EXP-AR-02 — Seven-condition matrix

Every group's natural phase contains:

- X1 AutoGen;
- X2 MetaGPT;
- X3 A2A;
- X4 MCP;
- X5 RAG;
- X6 MemoryBank;
- X7 LongLLMLingua;

crossed with T1-T3.

---

## EXP-AR-03 — Trajectory-level and group-level analysis are distinct

Trajectory-level analysis reconstructs concrete node/edge process paths.

Group-level analysis asks whether a structure:

- recurs;
- disappears;
- changes carrier;
- changes semantic form;
- migrates between C/P/R dimensions;
- closes earlier/later;
- re-enters after apparent resolution.

Neither level substitutes for the other.

---

## EXP-AR-04 — Unified observation chain

Forward analysis follows:

`source -> carrier -> transformation -> downstream read/adoption -> decision/action -> consequence`.

A report must state which links are directly observed and which remain not established.

---

## EXP-AR-05 — Complete-route principle

Primary cases preserve the complete recorded route from first recorded actor/event to terminal or censored stop.

Semantic target nodes are overlays on that route, not replacements for it.

---

## EXP-AR-06 — Source and descendant semantics are distinct

The audit must distinguish:

- source statement/state;
- direct copy;
- transformed descendant;
- translated/reframed descendant;
- later adopted premise;
- derived decision constraint.

A source remaining uncertain does not prevent a descendant from acquiring stronger operational authority.

---

## EXP-AR-07 — Visibility and adoption are distinct

An item being retrieved, recalled, compressed, transmitted, or present in context does not prove it affected a decision.

Use explicit relation classes such as:

- MERE_VISIBILITY;
- READ;
- ADOPTION;
- TRANSFORMATION;
- DECISION_APPLICATION;
- FINALIZATION;
- NOT_ESTABLISHED.

---

## EXP-AR-08 — Closure is multi-layered

At minimum distinguish:

1. functional execution state;
2. persisted repository/artifact state;
3. semantic account of completion;
4. process termination/closure.

These layers may diverge.

---

## EXP-AR-09 — CPR is dynamic

C, P, and R are not static tags.

They are assigned only through observed state change, boundary expansion, temporal carry-forward, descendant adoption, reactivation, or related process evidence.

---

## EXP-AR-10 — Cross-penetration requires a real path

C->P, P->C, C->R, R->P, and other cross-penetration claims require an actual recorded path connecting the dimensions.

Conceptual plausibility alone is insufficient.

---

## EXP-AR-11 — Same output does not imply same process

Two trajectories may reach equivalent user-facing outcomes through materially different route structures.

Those process differences are reportable without automatically labeling one route inferior.

---

## EXP-AR-12 — Different process does not imply harm

Process divergence becomes a risk claim only when evidence shows unsupported authority change, scope drift, non-closure, lineage loss, boundary violation, misleading semantic closure, or another explicitly defined consequence.

---

# 4. G1 diagnostic classification for forward use

G1 provides two protocol-level diagnostics that motivate v2.

## EXP-G1-01 — Historical T1 interface-dominated row

G1 T1 X2-X7 are classified as interface-dominated diagnostic trajectories because the v1 model-visible action-envelope description and parser-required type-tagged envelope were misaligned.

The 32-turn stop is the terminal boundary, but not the primary cause of meaningful-task non-completion.

These cells are retained for interface-methodology evidence and layer-specific pre-parser observations, not clean task-performance comparison.

---

## EXP-G1-02 — T2/T3 horizon censoring

After prospective action-contract clarification, T2/T3 exposed sustained valid execution.

Across the 14 T2/T3 cells:

- 2 provider-format failures were preserved separately;
- among the remaining 12 runnable trajectories, 10 hit the fixed 32-turn horizon;
- several had already written the requested artifact or made the key repair;
- X4-T3 did not naturally finalize until turn 31.

This supports increasing the forward common observation horizon before G2 rather than treating the 32-turn boundary as a natural closure window.

# 5. Relationship to the Engineering General Chapter

The Experimental General Chapter governs natural execution and interpretation.

The Engineering General Chapter governs:

- passive monitoring;
- structural warning;
- lineage localization;
- checkpoint/resumability;
- repair eligibility;
- structured repair package;
- one-shot local repair;
- repair-boundary watch;
- post-repair process comparison.

Natural membership and repair eligibility are separate.

Therefore:

> **All X1-X7 remain in every natural group. A system may later be ineligible for B without being removed from the experiment.**

This distinction is especially important for X1 and X3.
