## 1. Natural Formation: Three Domains, Three Concrete Semantic Forms

| Domain | Source meaning | Downstream descendant meaning | Mechanism reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finance | unconfirmed concentration-cap / portfolio status | booking gate -> compliance escalation -> portfolio non-clearance -> conditioned approval | authority status becomes an executable permission boundary |
| Supply chain | logistics capacity and no true internal surplus | external replenishment plan -> committed-demand boundary -> 9-day supplier timing -> monitored allocation v3 | capacity meaning becomes sourcing and timing policy |
| Software engineering | telemetry denominator gap / low-sample checkout signal | GO_CONDITIONAL_HOLD -> release authorization boundary -> SRE hold/rollback policy | statistical insufficiency becomes a deployment gate |

These examples do not assert that the domains are semantically identical. The shared mechanism is narrower: meaning changes form as it moves through specialized Agents, and the later decision may depend on a transformed descendant rather than on literal recurrence of the source text.

## 2. Minimal Perturbation: R5 Tests Dependence Without Repairing Shared State

| Realized path | R5 local operation | Observed semantic response | Mechanism family |

The probabilistic nature of the model is preserved rather than averaged away by canonical same-parent repetition.

## 6. What the First Batch Supports

The combined evidence supports a process-reality account in which multi-Agent coordination can create operationally active semantic descendants. Natural runs show how meaning becomes a gate, plan or control policy. R5 shows that removing one local authority status does not produce one fixed downstream direction. R6 shows why the ancestry of persistence matters: a semantic descendant can keep a prior relation active even after the direct stimulus is gone, whereas independent evidence can reconstruct the same endpoint without source-specific inertia.
