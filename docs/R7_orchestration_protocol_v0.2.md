## 1. Continuity with v0.1

R7 v0.1 asked whether orchestration structure changes trajectory-level Reality Bias dynamics through a Free Routing vs Structured Routing comparison.

v0.2 does not declare that question wrong. It clarifies the mechanism role of the structured condition:

> structured routing is interpreted as a temporary, dynamic **recovery-routing layer** used to trace, revise and repeatedly control a source-backed risk-bearing field lineage after a Jump has been localized.

The comparison is not a claim that fixed workflow orchestration is universally better than free multi-Agent organization.

## 2. Core R7 question

> Can sustained, lineage-aware structured feedback make risk-bearing system inertia traceable, locally repairable and controllable across downstream propagation while preserving unaffected process freedom?

R7 tests **controllability**. R5 tests one-shot perturbability. R6 characterizes post-reorganization inertia.

## 3. Why R7 cannot be a one-shot repair

System inertia is defined by historical inheritance across later context, messages, state, invocations, Agent activations and descendants.

A single local correction at J0 may change the path but cannot by itself establish that the already distributed downstream carriers have been controlled.

Therefore active ALR must be evaluated as a feedback process rather than as `repair once -> declare recovery`.

## 4. Natural/free condition

The reference natural condition preserves the Free-Agent Arena principle:

- one overall task goal;
- registered specialist pool;
- Agents may dynamically communicate/invoke under the frozen Arena policy;
- no ALR active recovery routing is enabled unless the experimental condition explicitly activates it;
- passive structural observability remains enabled so evidence capacity is comparable across conditions.

The natural condition may still use common action interfaces and role/responsibility definitions. The relevant invariant is that ALR does not prescribe the realized topology.

## 5. Active ALR condition

An active treatment begins only after a preregistered/frozen recovery anchor exists.

Required stages:

1. **Locate** — identify J0 and the source-backed risk-bearing field lineage.
2. **Structure** — derive the affected dependency closure and preserved/unaffected set.
3. **Repair** — revise the target field/state under the named authority/provenance condition.
4. **Propagate** — route revised state through the affected recovery scope with revision identity/provenance.
5. **Observe** — measure residual old inertia, recurrence, transfer, secondary Jump and downstream topology.
6. **Re-repair** — if a preregistered residual/recurrence trigger remains, perform another bounded recovery round.
7. **Release** — stop active recovery routing when the preregistered release condition is satisfied; resume free routing.

This loop may be written:

**Locate -> Structure -> Repair -> Propagate -> Observe -> Re-repair -> Release**

## 6. Recovery routing is dynamic, not a fixed workflow template

The historical v0.1 structured sequence remains valid as a historical/engineering orchestration condition. It must not be silently presented as the final ALR mechanism.

Forward ALR recovery routing should, where runtime support permits, be generated from the realized affected lineage rather than from one permanent sequence independent of J0.

Two different recovery anchors may therefore produce different recovery topologies.

## 7. Field-level control object

The primary control object is not merely an Agent identity.

At minimum the recovery record should identify:

- target field/state key;
- source Jump/event;
- prior revision/state;
- repaired revision/state;
- parent revision;
- affected descendant relations;
- affected Agents/nodes;
- preserved Agents/nodes;
- recovery round;
- current residual/recurrence state.

An Agent may participate in both affected and unaffected work. Do not mark its entire history as contaminated without source-backed dependency evidence.

## 8. Conditions and comparators

The minimum forward comparison may retain:

- **Condition F — Free continuation:** passive observability, no active recovery routing;
- **Condition A — ALR structured recovery:** same observation substrate plus active lineage-aware recovery routing.

Where mechanism separation is useful, an additional comparator may be preregistered:

- **Condition P — Experiment-maintained field persistence:** the same semantic field/signal remains continuously available without ALR dependency-closure recovery logic.

If Condition P is used, match semantic payload as closely as possible. The intended contrast is propagation/recovery structure, not richer correction content in ALR.

Condition P is experiment-maintained persistence, not evidence of endogenous inertia.

## 9. Common observability requirement

All conditions must use materially equivalent passive structural evidence capture.

A finding that ALR is “more traceable” is invalid if only the ALR condition records lineage/provenance needed to make the comparison.

Differences in active routing/revision state must be treatment differences; differences in basic evidence capture should be minimized and explicitly reported.

## 10. Primary structural outcomes

### Risk-control outcomes

- risk-bearing descendant reach/depth;
- residual risk-bearing field state;
- recurrence/rebound;
- secondary Jump formation;
- affected Agent count;
- old-lineage survival after each recovery round;
- recovery rounds to containment/release;
- post-release recurrence within the observed window.

### Freedom-preservation outcomes

- unaffected nodes preserved;
- unaffected fields preserved;
- unaffected branches preserved;
- unnecessary reopen count;
- whether full rerun was avoided or invoked;
- non-risk Agent participation retained;
- non-risk path-family diversity retained;
- active recovery scope relative to the realized graph.

R7 cannot define success solely as reduced activity or a standardized path.

## 11. Release condition

A real R7 protocol must freeze a release rule before execution.

Possible structural components include:

- all currently reachable affected descendants have received/recomputed the required field revision;
- no source-backed unresolved risk-bearing descendant remains inside the active closure;
- no recurrence trigger is observed during a named post-repair observation window;
- unresolved/censored states are explicitly preserved rather than declared repaired.

Release ends active structured recovery routing. It does not guarantee permanent absence of later recurrence.

## 12. Proposal versus realization

Where the recovery policy blocks or redirects a subject proposal, retain both:

- the original subject proposal;
- the realized action admitted by the recovery policy.

Do not erase evidence that the model attempted an action that the recovery layer did not realize.

This remains a useful principle from v0.1.

## 13. Semantic boundary

R7 establishes structural recovery/control evidence only.

Whether J0, a descendant, a recurrence or a recovery event is semantic C/P/R is adjudicated in R8 from frozen evidence.

## 14. No terminal-answer target

ALR must not be given a desired final answer merely to force convergence.

The treatment changes lineage-aware recovery structure and field revision/propagation conditions. The downstream Agent process remains generative within the allowed recovery scope.

## 15. Offline implementation path

Before any real provider execution, add and test:

- field-revision identity and lineage support;
- affected/preserved closure derivation;
- recovery-round ledger;
- dynamic recovery-routing plan;
- proposal-versus-realization preservation;
- release-condition evaluator;
- same-observability validation across conditions;
- regression tests proving natural/free routing remains unchanged when active recovery is disabled.

## 16. Real-run gate

A future real R7 run requires a new run-freeze artifact containing:

- exact condition definitions and hashes;
- target J0/field selection rule;
- dependency-closure version;
- recovery-routing version;
- release rule;
- horizon and censoring rules;
- primary risk-control and freedom-preservation metrics;
- model/provider binding;
- repeat count;
- explicit paid authorization and spending ceiling.

This protocol authorizes no provider call.
