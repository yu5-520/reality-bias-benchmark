## Publication role

The A–F sequence belongs in the engineering extension of the process-reality
paper. It is a mechanism series, not a new repair-success benchmark.

The sequence is:

`local repair -> graph-informed carrier repair -> obligation repair ->
coordinated route repair -> hard authority -> bounded verification/closure`.

## Results wording

The historical local repair showed that changing a target did not reliably
repair the subsequent process. We therefore increased repair strength while
holding the frozen case and downstream observation logic fixed.

A graph-informed application repair narrowed downstream mutation but did not
remove temporal or collaboration inertia. Repairing four live pending obligations
then changed the immediate behavior, yet equivalent task pressure regenerated
through descendant messages. A coordinated complete-route cut jointly
superseded the stale obligations, duplicate scheduling and current completion
state. This suppressed reimplementation: the branch made no further application
writes, but review and verification pressure still regenerated and consumed the
remaining horizon.

We next kept the coordinated cut fixed and bound the post-repair branch to a hard
capability envelope. All observed attempts to reopen collaboration were blocked
from taking native effect and the final pending queue fell from 18 to 5, but the
system still spent the full remaining horizon repeatedly reading and testing the
already-correct implementation.

Finally, we kept both the coordinated repair and hard authority fixed and bounded
post-repair verification. Each retained role received one evidence-gathering
batch, a required disposition and at most one adaptation call. The trajectory
ended after 11 logical subject responses, rather than 40, with no application
write and an empty final queue. Four roles produced dispositions. Release lead
still attempted to reopen specialist work after its verification budget; instead
of allowing another loop, the branch ended at an explicit
`ESCALATION_REQUIRED` boundary.

The mechanism result is therefore layered rather than binary: complete graph
evidence improves repair only when it is translated into coordinated carrier
changes, persistent execution authority and a bounded closure rule.

## Discussion wording

The complete graph should not be interpreted as a license to edit every connected
node. Its value is to identify the supported authority route and the smallest
coordinated set of carriers required to change that route. The contrasts show
why successful local mutation is insufficient: descendants can reconstruct an
obligation, and even after scope expansion is blocked, allowed verification can
preserve temporal inertia.

The final engineering principle is:

> repair the supported route, bind post-repair authority, bound verification,
> and escalate unresolved work explicitly rather than allowing silent scope
> growth or indefinite rechecking.

This is not a claim of universal autonomous repair success.

## Methods boundary

No Natural-A trajectory or frozen control arm was rerun to search for a favorable
result. The A–F contrasts use frozen controls and one-shot treatment branches.

F's first provider response was produced once in workflow `37421493309`.
A harness failure occurred after that response but before its actions took native
effect. The exact request and response were content-addressed and reused without
provider recall when the branch was resumed. Two subsequent technical failures
occurred before any new provider response. The completed trajectory in workflow
`37422418453` therefore consists of the original frozen first response plus ten
new subsequent responses.

Do not fold A–F into the historical 45-pair denominator or convert them into a
repair success rate.
